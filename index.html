Product goal

Expand M360’s Distribution materials experience to support Substation materials.

The product team knew the symptom. I needed to define the problem.

What I heard
Each role saw a different part of the truth.
Substation Designer
“I’m out of the loop once procurement takes over.”
Purchasing
“I send it, but they don’t know where to find it after. Then I get asked again—what’s on this PO?”
Depot
“We open the Connex and it’s not what the BoM say—it’s Cantex instead of Carlon. Now we’re chasing the truth.”
Each stakeholder group only saw a different part of the truth.
How I synthesized it
I traced the same material state across roles
Compare accountsWhat each role believed happened
Trace artifactsMaterial List, PO, email, receiving information
Follow state changesWhat changed, when, and who knew
Look for handoff failureWhere information stopped propagating
I didn’t try to reconcile the stories into one version of truth. I treated disagreement itself as evidence of a broken handoff.
What I surfaced
The breakdown became visible at substitution
Substation Designer specifies A
A unavailable
Purchasing substitutes B
Purchasing’s information changes
Downstream Material List may still show A

At a system level, this is a cross-role handoff failure.

Insight 01 — Changed Scope

Unlike distribution, substation materials follow two distinct workflows, and it is the minor materials that create the bigger pain.

ImpactScope changed

Focusing the initial product scope on minor materials.

My recommendation

Prioritize the minor-material workflow for the Substation scope, rather than forcing major and minor materials into one product flow.

Decision impact

Product and Business narrowed the scope to minor materials, where the research showed greater operational friction and more immediate product value.

Insight 02 — Changed User/workflow model

Purchasing is a core actor in the minor-material workflow — not a supporting stakeholder.

My recommendation

Bring Purchasing into the core user model and design the workflow around Designer → Purchasing → Depot.

Decision impact

Purchasing became a core user group, changing the workflow model from Designer → Depot to
Designer → Purchasing → Depot.

Insight 03 — Added Workflow requirement

Material substitutions are where minor material process breaks across roles.

My recommendation

User needs a live, centralized source of truth (material list) tied to each individual substation project that supports material substitution.

Decision impact

Product/Design made substitution handling a core requirement.

Research moved the team from extending an existing experience to designing for the actual Substation material workflow.
